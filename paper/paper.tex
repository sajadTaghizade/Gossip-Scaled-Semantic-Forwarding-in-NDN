\begin{abstract}
Named Data Networking drops an Interest whose name does not match a
forwarding-table entry, so two organisations that name the same sensor
differently cannot reach each other's data. Embedding names and matching them by
cosine similarity fixes that, at the cost of running a sentence encoder in the
forwarding path. Per-router caching does not amortise that cost: split the same
traffic across more edge routers and each cache sees a thinner slice, so over a
short horizon the network re-runs the encoder \emph{more} as it grows. We share
a resolution over anti-entropy gossip once, and only once a returned Data packet
has proved it. Over a 60\,s run at 16 edge routers this holds the growth in
encoder invocations to $+9.9\%$ against a per-router cache's $+59.6\%$, removing
$27.7\%$ of inferences; we also measure the two conditions that bound the
claim, since the saving falls to $6.5\%$ over 600\,s and reverses below about
four edge routers. Two further results follow from the same verification
signal. First, the feedback a forwarding plane calibrates against is
\emph{biased} rather than merely noisy: a producer's refusal conflates ``this
name is not mine'' with ``this name is mine but not in that wording'', and we
trace the conflation to a concrete protocol gap---NDN's Nack has no equivalent
of HTTP's 406 or DNS's NODATA. Second, because a gossiped mapping is a
falsifiable claim, a router can audit its peers using verdicts it already
collects; this attenuates a persistent poisoning attacker by a factor that
\emph{grows} with attack intensity, from $1.2\times$ at the lowest injection
rate to $2.0\times$ at the highest. We report an attenuation, not a breakdown
bound: the rate-invariance such a bound predicts was tested and did not hold.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Named Data Networking (NDN)~\cite{jacobson2009ccn,zhang2014ndn} forwards on
names, and a name that does not match the Forwarding Information Base (FIB) is
dropped. A client asking for \ndn{/hospital/temp-sensor/room-101} gets nothing
from a network that advertises
\ndn{/smart-hospital/floor-1/temperature/room-101}, though the data it wants is
one hop away. Amadeo \emph{et al.}~\cite{amadeo2026} answer this by embedding
names and forwarding on cosine similarity, caching each resolution in an
\emph{Embedding Store} at the router that made it.

That design leaves a scaling problem its own conclusion names as future work.
A per-router cache is populated by the traffic that router sees. Add edge
routers without adding traffic and every cache thins, so the network pays for
more encoder invocations in total. Our measurements put the effect at
$+59.6\%$ more inferences from 1 to 16 edge routers on one catalog and
$+67.4\%$ on the other, for identical offered load.

This paper's premise is that the fix is not a bigger cache but a shared one,
and that NDN already supplies what makes sharing safe. A semantic match is a
guess; a returned Data packet proves it. Gossiping only proven mappings means a
wrong guess never propagates, which is what lets the similarity threshold be
permissive without the usual cost.

\textbf{Contributions.} We claim three things, each scoped to what we measured.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Sharing, not caching, is what keeps recognition cost from growing
with the network} (\S\ref{sec:scaling}). We also measure the two conditions
that bound this: the advantage decays with run length, and reverses below about
four edge routers.
\item \emph{The free labels a forwarding plane calibrates on are biased}
(\S\ref{sec:bias}), and the bias has a nameable protocol cause rather than
being noise. We found and corrected an instance of it in our own producer
model, and report the regime in which correcting it helps and the regime in
which it does not.
\item \emph{A verifying forwarding plane can audit its own peers}
(\S\ref{sec:threat}). Byzantine-robust gossip normally cannot detect a liar and
must settle for bounding its influence; a gossiped mapping is falsifiable, so
detection is available from verdicts already collected.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What we do not claim.} Edge tagging---annotating an Interest with the
resolved prefix so later hops skip the encoder---is Amadeo \emph{et al.}'s
mechanism, inherited here. Anti-entropy dataset synchronisation is a decade old
in NDN~\cite{zhu2013chronosync,hoque2013nlsr}; our gossip layer is that
mechanism carrying a different payload. Learning per-item decision boundaries
under an error budget is what vCache~\cite{schroeder2025vcache} does for LLM
prompt caching. We evaluated an error budget as a replacement for the
similarity threshold, it lost to a threshold tuned on one catalog and carried to
another, and we report that as a withdrawn claim (\S\ref{sec:risk}).

\textbf{Organisation.} Section~\ref{sec:related} places this work against
semantic forwarding in ICN, the synchronisation layers NDN already provides,
and the literature on learned boundaries and Byzantine-robust gossip.
Section~\ref{sec:method} describes the simulator, the producer model and the
measured cost basis. Sections~\ref{sec:scaling}, \ref{sec:bias}
and~\ref{sec:threat} present the three contributions, with
Section~\ref{sec:risk} covering the claim we withdrew;
Section~\ref{sec:supporting} reports supporting results and
Section~\ref{sec:limits} the limitations.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Semantic forwarding in ICN.} Fuzzy Interest
Forwarding~\cite{chan2017fuzzy} matches name components with Word2Vec and is the
original statement of the problem. Amadeo \emph{et al.}~\cite{amadeo2026} use a
sentence encoder, settle on a threshold of $0.7$, and evaluate a single access
router. INF-NDN~\cite{raza2024infndn} routes topic tags through central
supernodes---a \emph{competing} answer that concentrates semantic resolution
rather than sharing it.

\textbf{A note on ``SAF''.} In NDN, SAF already denotes Stochastic Adaptive
Forwarding~\cite{posch2017saf}. We write ``semantic forwarding'' or name the
authors; our result files retain the key \texttt{saf} for continuity.

\textbf{Synchronisation NDN already has.} The anti-entropy and rumour-spreading
pair we use is the classical epidemic construction of Demers \emph{et
al.}~\cite{demers1987epidemic}, whose $O(\log N)$ propagation under random peer
choice~\cite{karp2000rumor} is what Section~\ref{sec:frontier} preserves by
keeping one uniform pick. ChronoSync~\cite{zhu2013chronosync} and its successors
reconcile datasets by exchanging condensed digests, and
NLSR~\cite{hoque2013nlsr} already floods name-prefix reachability over such a
layer; Moll \emph{et al.}~\cite{moll2022sok} survey how these designs differ.
Our contribution is not the mechanism but the payload and the precondition: a
wording-to-route mapping, shared only after a Data packet proved it.
Section~\ref{sec:scaling} measures an approximation of an unbounded sync layer
precisely because ``why not just announce the prefix'' is the first objection
this design invites.

\textbf{Learned boundaries under an error bound.}
vCache~\cite{schroeder2025vcache} learns per-item thresholds under a user-set
error bound for prompt caching; Finamore \emph{et
al.}~\cite{finamore2022approximatekey} do error-controlled similarity caching in
a networking setting. The boundaries themselves are conformal in the sense of
Vovk \emph{et al.}~\cite{vovk2005alrw}; pooling calibration across parties is
federated conformal prediction~\cite{lu2023federated}, and the drifting variant
we evaluate follows adaptive conformal inference~\cite{gibbs2021aci}. That a
threshold tuned on one catalog need not hold on another is the covariate-shift
problem Tibshirani \emph{et al.}~\cite{tibshirani2019covariate} treat formally.
What is specific to NDN is that the labels are free: an LLM cache needs a judge
model to decide whether a hit was correct, which is the expense one was trying
to avoid, whereas a forwarding plane receives Data or a Nack either way.

\textbf{Biased feedback.} Section~\ref{sec:bias} is an instance of
missing-not-at-random feedback, the pattern Saito \emph{et
al.}~\cite{saito2020unbiased} treat in implicit-feedback recommendation: the
label a route receives depends on a producer's declared vocabulary, which is
also what determines whether the request could have been served. Our
contribution is locating it in a forwarding plane and identifying the protocol
gap that causes it.

\textbf{Byzantine-robust gossip.} Robust-aggregation methods (clipping,
trimming, bounded per-peer contribution) bound an adversary's influence rather
than identifying it, because in gossip learning a neighbour ships a gradient no
honest node can check. Section~\ref{sec:threat} exploits the fact that a
gossiped \emph{mapping} can be checked.

\section{System Model and Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Simulator}

Names are embedded with MiniLM~\cite{wang2020minilm}, a distilled
Sentence-BERT model~\cite{reimers2019sbert} and the one the baseline settles on.
Each router has a single-server FIFO queue, so a strategy that adds work to the
forwarding path pays in queueing delay rather than in a configuration constant.
A strategy is consulted at exactly one point---the FIB miss---and told the
outcome afterwards; Content Store, PIT and exact FIB lookup are identical across
strategies, so a difference in results is a difference in policy.

Similarity may choose a \emph{route}, never decide that two names denote the
same object: a semantic hit in the Content Store would hand a client data it did
not request, and one in the PIT would return data down the wrong chain.

\subsection{Costs are measured, not assumed}

Every latency constant is measured on the machine running the study and
recorded with a flag saying whether it was measured or assumed. One MiniLM-L6
inference costs $7.045$\,ms. A cosine search over the 50-entry FIB costs
$0.0045$\,ms. The ratio is about $1566$:$1$, so \emph{the encoder is the only
cost that matters}: any scheme that optimises the similarity \emph{search}
optimises a rounding error. This is why our mechanism targets the number of
inferences rather than their individual cost.

\subsection{Producers are modelled, not oracular}

Letting a producer consult ground truth would be the weakest assumption
available, so instead each holds a \emph{declared schema}: the instance it is
attached to and the metric terms it answers to, as a service registry or OpenAPI
description would provide. Declarations are deliberately incomplete---at
coverage $0.7$ a producer leaves $29\%$ of the wordings it can be asked by
undeclared and refuses requests genuinely meant for it. The feedback channel is
informative rather than correct, which is the situation any deployment is in.

\subsection{Workload and metrics}

Requests are Poisson arrivals over a Zipf ($\alpha=1.2$) popularity
distribution, with each consumer population drawing from an overlapping but
distinct slice of the catalog---without that overlap parameter, sharing would be
either trivially redundant or trivially useless. Each catalog carries 50
services, 300 rewordings and 350 distractors that no service can satisfy, so
precision is measurable.

Scoring is correctness-aware: every Interest ends in exactly one of
\emph{correct}, \emph{misdelivered}, \emph{rejected} or \emph{timeout}, judged
against ground truth the routers never see. Since distractors are supposed to be
rejected, a single accuracy number would reward a strategy that answers nothing,
so precision, recall and false-positive rate are reported separately.

\subsection{Comparison basis}
\label{sec:basis}

We reproduce the baseline's evaluation setting rather than inventing one: the
access-edge topology, the one-way latencies (10\,ms client-to-router, 20\,ms
router-to-provider), the 50-entry FIB, the 30--300 Interests/s range, the Zipf
skew of $1.2$, a disabled Content Store and an unbounded PIT all come from
Amadeo \emph{et al.}~\cite{amadeo2026}, and our catalogs share their
construction exactly---50 services, one canonical name and six rewordings each,
350 satisfiable names, plus an equal number of unrelated ones.

We report their metrics. Their \emph{service discovery time} is our
Interest-satisfaction time, decomposing identically; their \emph{processing
time} is our encoder-invocation count times the measured inference cost, plus
queueing. Their \emph{share of Interests forwarded on the first attempt} is our
satisfaction rate, with one deliberate difference: they count an Interest
successful when it is forwarded and answered, while we also require the Data to
have come from the service the name should resolve to. A confident match to the
wrong room's sensor is a success under the first definition and a misdelivery
under ours, and a metric that cannot see that failure cannot be tuned against
it.

\textbf{Absolute numbers are therefore not comparable across the two papers,
and we do not compare them.} They report essentially complete forwarding at
$\mathrm{Th}=0.7$; ours yields recall of $0.866$ and $0.804$ there. The gap is
in the rewordings, not the method---theirs are synonym substitutions, ours also
drop hierarchy levels, reorder and abbreviate, and a seventh family draws on
published IoT vocabularies. Every comparison here is between strategies inside
one harness, never against a published number.

\subsection{Cross-validation}

A hand-written simulator can be self-consistent and still model NDN
incorrectly, so we cross-check the transport layer---the part that is \emph{not}
our contribution---against ndnSIM on the same topology, delays and request
pattern with semantic resolution disabled. Median round-trip times agree to
within $0.11$\,ms once the $1$\,ms producer service time our model charges is
accounted for. All results are 20 seeded runs with 95\% confidence intervals,
and are reproducible across processes: three defects in which an
interpreter-salted iteration order reached the event queue were found and fixed
during development, and a regression test compares metrics from two child
processes run under differing \texttt{PYTHONHASHSEED} values.

\section{Scaling: Sharing versus Caching}
\label{sec:scaling}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig_scaling.pdf}
\caption{Encoder invocations against edge routers. The per-router cache grows
with the network; gossiped sharing stays nearly flat.}
\label{fig:scaling}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling} is the primary result. Semantic forwarding without a
cache pays for every FIB miss, so it has nothing to erode and grows $+2.8\%$
and $+3.1\%$ from 1 to 16 edge routers. The per-router cache thins as routers
multiply and grows $+59.6\%$ on hospital and $+67.4\%$ on city. Sharing holds
that to $+9.9\%$ and $+17.3\%$, removing $27.7\%$ and $28.3\%$ of all
inferences at 16 edge routers. The claim is independent of any threshold or
error budget: it is a property of how many times the encoder runs.

\subsection{Two conditions that bound it}

\textbf{The horizon.} A cold cache costs one inference per router per wording:
$N$ routers pay it $N$ times, but they pay it once. Over 600\,s instead of
60\,s the per-router cache grows $+14.4\%$ rather than $+55.2\%$, and gossip's
saving at 16 edge routers falls from $25.6\%$ to $6.5\%$ (city: $26.8\%$ to
$9.2\%$). The ordering never reverses, but the magnitude does. Sharing pays a
network that is large and still learning its catalog.

\textbf{Network size.} At a single edge router there is nobody to share with,
and anti-entropy costs $4$--$5\%$ more inferences than a plain per-router
cache. The benefit appears from about four edge routers upward.

\subsection{Is the decay an artifact of a closed catalog?}

A natural hypothesis is that the decay is an artifact of a finite catalog:
once every router has met all 300 rewordings there is nothing left to learn. We
tested it by introducing wordings over the run instead of making them all
askable at $t=0$, drawn from an independent random stream so only \emph{which}
wording a request uses changes. The hypothesis is false, and instructively so: a
slower arrival rate lowers the saving at \emph{every} horizon rather than
sustaining it, from $25.6\%$ (closed) to $3.0\%$ (one new wording every 5\,s) at
60\,s, monotone across the grid and on both catalogs. Gossip's saving comes
from the \emph{backlog} of resolutions some routers hold and others do not, and
a closed catalog at $t=0$ is the largest backlog obtainable.

\subsection{Would a more aggressive sync layer do better?}

NDN already ships anti-entropy synchronisation, so the obvious objection is to
turn it on and drop this layer. We bound that with an arm in which every router
reconciles with every peer each round with no cap per exchange. It removes
\emph{fewer} inferences than the bounded protocol ($21.0\%$ against $25.6\%$ at
16 edge routers) while sending $1.2$--$1.6\times$ the bytes. Anti-entropy at
fanout 2 already reaches everyone in $O(\log N)$ rounds; what limits the saving
is how many distinct wordings the network has yet to see, which no amount of
synchronisation changes. The part of the design doing the work is \emph{what} is
shared and \emph{when}---a mapping, only once a Data packet proved it---not how
aggressively.

\subsection{A Pareto frontier, not an operating point}
\label{sec:frontier}

The gossip period is a degree of freedom, and a single value would hide a
trade worth seeing. Sweeping it from $0.25$ to $5$\,s at 16 edge routers moves
the saving from $24.4\%$ to $18.0\%$ while gossip traffic falls from $933$ to
$433$\,kB; at 4 edge routers the saving is flat across the same range
($8.5$--$9.6\%$) and a longer period is nearly free. The exchange rate between
bytes and inferences therefore depends on network size, and a conclusion drawn
at one operating point would have been wrong at the other. The chosen $0.5$\,s
is also Pareto-optimal among the points measured: $0.25$\,s is worse on
\emph{both} axes.

\textbf{Delta-biased peer selection.} Once converged, the modal anti-entropy
exchange is two agreeing digests and no payload. Ranking partners by how far
behind they are, read off the watermark the sender already keeps and costing no
messages, redirects those exchanges; one pick of \texttt{fanout} stays uniform,
since a purely greedy rule is no longer an epidemic process and can starve a
peer that never tops the ranking. This is the only mechanism we tested that
moves the frontier outward rather than along it: paired at 16 edge routers it
saves $11.1 \pm 7.5$ inferences while sending $3.68 \pm 0.96\%$ fewer bytes at
$0.5$\,s, and $24.9 \pm 7.3$ inferences at $1.0$\,s (20/20 seeds), with
satisfaction unchanged. The honest magnitude is $1$--$1.5\%$ of invocations and
$3\%$ of bytes, a refinement that costs nothing rather than a headline, and it
helps only mid-range, which is itself evidence it works for the stated reason.

\section{Calibrating from Free Labels}
\label{sec:risk}

A threshold is a property of the catalog it was tuned on: $0.7$ is chosen on
the baseline's catalog and yields recall of $0.866$ and $0.804$ on ours. Worse,
one number must serve every route---some services sit alone in embedding space
and a loose score is safe, others have near-identical siblings and a high score
is a coin flip. Since every semantic forwarding decision is an experiment the
network answers, $(\text{score},\text{verdict})$ pairs accumulate for free, and
an operator can set an error budget $\varepsilon$ instead of a threshold: for
route $r$, take the lowest score whose upper confidence bound on the error rate
among observations at or above it stays within $\varepsilon$. We use a Wilson
bound, since with a dozen observations the normal approximation assigns a route
with no errors a bound of exactly zero, and shrink each route toward a pooled
boundary at a rate set by its own evidence.

Two results follow, and only one of them is good.

\textbf{Risk control deadlocks without exploration.} A boundary set too high
blocks exactly the decisions that would produce the evidence to lower it. Left
unexplored the controller settles at refuse-everything and stays there, reaching
$0.732$ satisfaction at $\varepsilon=0.02$; tightening $\varepsilon$ further
changes nothing because it is already refusing what it can. Spending $5\%$ of
refused decisions on evidence reaches $0.818$. This is not an estimator bug but
a property of learning from one's own choices, and we report those exploratory
forwards separately because they are decisions the budget did not cover.

\textbf{The efficiency claim was tested and withdrawn.} The budget holds
out-of-sample in all fourteen tests. But against a threshold tuned on one
catalog and carried to the other, the controller Pareto-dominates in \emph{none}
of fourteen comparisons while the transferred threshold dominates in seven. What
survives is narrower: the controller needs no labelled sample of the domain it
runs on. An operator who \emph{has} one should tune a threshold instead.

\section{The Feedback is Biased, Not Just Noisy}
\label{sec:bias}

This is the finding the paper stands on alongside \S\ref{sec:scaling}, and it
emerged from a defect in our own producer model.

A producer refusing a semantically-resolved Interest has two distinct reasons,
and NDN's Nack cannot distinguish them. \emph{This name is not mine} is a
routing fact: the mapping that sent the Interest here is wrong. \emph{This name
is mine but not in that wording} is not: the route was correct and only the
phrasing fell outside what the producer declared. Collapsing the two makes every
gap in a producer's vocabulary look like evidence against a route that was in
fact right. HTTP separates 404 from 406 and DNS separates NXDOMAIN from NODATA
for this reason; semantic forwarding is what makes the distinction necessary in
NDN.

\textbf{The distinction must be one the producer can actually make.} Our first
implementation tested whether the producer published \emph{the prefix the router
chose}---a test a misroute satisfies by construction, since that is why the
Interest was routed there. Measured at full declaration coverage, where a
genuine wording gap is nearly impossible, 132 of 157 refusals reported
``unknown wording'' about a route that was simply wrong, 2 were the case the
label exists for, and the routing-error label never fired once. The channel
carried almost no information.

What a producer genuinely knows is which \emph{instance} it is attached to---the
room, the junction, the meter. A request that does not name that instance is not
for this service whatever the wording. That test is exact in our runs: every
instance mismatch was a real misroute or an unsatisfiable distractor, with no
false positives. It converts a dead channel into an informative one: at
coverage $0.5$, $291$ refusals are correctly labelled routing errors and $546$
are wording gaps, $71\%$ of them genuine.

\textbf{The residue is irreducible, and it inverts.} A producer serving
temperature in room 101, asked for humidity in room 101, cannot distinguish a
metric it does not serve from a synonym of its own it forgot to declare. The
base rate of genuine gaps among such refusals moves from $0.021$ at full
coverage to $0.711$ at half coverage---and the right policy inverts with it.
Ignoring ambiguous refusals gains $0.07$ satisfaction at coverage $0.5$
($0.844$ against $0.774$) and \emph{costs} $0.014$ at full coverage on city
($0.963$ against $0.978$). Declaration coverage is a property of other
organisations' service registries, which an operator does not observe.

We attempted to close this with inverse propensity weighting, estimating the
propensity per route. It failed, and the failure is informative: the estimated
weight sat between $0.414$ and $0.470$ in \emph{every} regime and both catalogs,
because a correct route at low coverage both serves often and is refused often,
so a route-conditioned ratio barely moves. The quantity that does move is a
single network-wide base rate. We report the regime inversion as an open
problem with its magnitude measured, rather than as a mechanism that closes it.

\section{Threat Model and Peer Reputation}
\label{sec:threat}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig_robust.pdf}
\caption{Satisfaction and realised error under a persistent poisoning attacker.
Reputation is free at zero compromise and recovers most of the attack's cost
thereafter.}
\label{fig:robust}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Threat model}

\textbf{What the attacker controls.} A fixed share of routers, chosen uniformly
at random, each a full participant holding real keys and peer relationships---so
nothing here is defeated by authenticating the channel. It behaves correctly on
the forwarding path and lies only into gossip; a router that drops traffic is a
denial-of-service problem the ICN literature already
treats~\cite{ghali2014poisoning} and would confound the measurement.

\textbf{What it can do.} Inject fabricated mappings and fabricated calibration
evidence, re-injecting both every round so that retraction after one round trip
settles nothing. It knows the catalog well enough to target names clients
actually request, which is the strong assumption here.

\textbf{What it cannot do.} Forge another router's identity, partition the
network, observe traffic it does not carry, or make a producer answer a name it
does not publish. This boundary matters for \S\ref{sec:bias}: the refusal reason
is authored by the producer, so a compromised \emph{router} cannot forge one,
and what a compromised \emph{producer} could do is unmeasured.

\textbf{What limits damage structurally.} A router's own confirmed mappings
outrank anything it is told. That is not a defence added against this attack; it
falls out of gossiping only what a returned Data packet proved.

\subsection{Verification-grounded reputation}

Byzantine-robust gossip normally defends without ground truth and must bound an
adversary's influence rather than identify it. A gossiped mapping is different:
it is falsifiable, and the producer answers it. We charge each refusal to the
peer that supplied the mapping and never reinstall a pair the router disproved
first-hand. Trust is a Wilson \emph{lower} bound on a peer's success rate,
mirroring the upper bound on error in \S\ref{sec:risk}---both bound in the
direction that costs us. A peer with no history is believed, deliberately:
distrusting unaudited peers would break the bootstrap gossip exists for.

Calibration evidence gets a different defence, since an observation about a
route is not immediately falsifiable: no single peer may occupy more than a
quarter of a route's calibration window, bounding contribution by construction
rather than by detection.

\subsection{An attenuation, not a breakdown bound}

It is tempting to formalise the residual as a bound. Each pair of an honest
router and a compromised neighbour admits at most $k$ mappings before the liar
is cut off, where $k$ is the trust prior, giving admitted poison
$\lesssim 2Ef(1-f)k$ for $E$ adjacencies and compromised fraction $f$---%
quadratic in $f$, and \emph{independent of injection rate}.

We tested both halves. The mechanism half holds: sweeping $k$ over
$\{1,2,4,8,16\}$ moves surviving poison monotonically from $494$ to $1067$, so
the admission fee is real and is the knob. \textbf{Rate invariance fails.}
Taking the attacker from 1 to 16 fabricated mappings per round raises the
defended arm's realised error by $1.43\times$ on hospital and $1.91\times$ on
city. A bound counted in pairs does not move with rate; this moves. We therefore
do not claim a breakdown point.

What survives is an attenuation whose value \emph{grows} with attack intensity.
The undefended arm rises $2.35\times$ and $3.25\times$ over the same range, so
the ratio between defended and undefended widens from $1.24\times$ at the
lowest rate to $2.04\times$ at the highest on hospital, and $1.72\times$ to
$2.93\times$ on city. The defence is worth more the harder it is attacked.

The sweep also selects a better prior than intuition did. A fee of $k=1$ is best
under attack but falsely distrusts $10.9$ honest peers on a clean network and
degrades sharing by $3\%$; $k=2$ distrusts $1.1$ for $0.06\%$, while realising
$0.037$ error against $k=4$'s $0.044$. We use $k=2$. The honest-network control
is what makes that visible---the attack column alone would have argued for $1$.

\section{Supporting Results}
\label{sec:supporting}

\textbf{Latency and energy.} Encoding on every FIB miss saturates the
forwarding path: p95 round-trip time rises from $79$ to $172$\,ms between 30 and
300 Interests/s, while cached and gossiped variants stay flat at $78$--$83$\,ms.
The knee sits just past 200 Interests/s, independently reproducing the
bottleneck the baseline reports beyond roughly 210 for the same encoder---%
agreement we did not tune for, and evidence the cost model is calibrated. On the
radio model of Askar \emph{et al.}~\cite{askar2024sef}, with encoder energy
charged at measured CPU draw, GS-NDN uses $634$\,J against $735$\,J uncached and
$654$\,J for a per-router cache, at higher satisfaction. A character $n$-gram
control reaches $0.791$ satisfaction where MiniLM reaches $0.969$: it resolves
abbreviations and fails on true synonymy, which is what the encoder pays for.

\textbf{Schema drift.} Producer relocation hurts every strategy equally, since
FIB withdrawal invalidates a stale mapping before any producer can refuse it.
Schema drift---a producer quietly narrowing what it answers to, with no routing
event---removes that confound, and there verification's advantage appears:
$+0.0042 \pm 0.0018$ satisfaction on hospital (16/20 seeds) and
$+0.0124 \pm 0.0045$ on city (19/20), paired seed by seed. Nothing in the
routing plane observes a schema drift; feedback is the only signal that can.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{Our synonyms are easier than standardised ones.} The catalogs are
generated from a lexicon we wrote. To test whether that lexicon flatters the
encoder, we added rewordings transcribed from Brick, SAREF, Project Haystack and
W3C SSN/SOSA. They are markedly harder: on city, rank-1 recognition is $0.578$
for ontology-derived wordings against $0.920$ for ours. This \emph{confirms} the
concern rather than removing it, and every recognition number here should be
read as an upper bound.

\textbf{Provenance is not implemented.} Signing an observation so a lie can be
attributed is the other defence our threat model names, and it is absent. A
deployment facing this threat model needs it; we show the attack lands and by
how much, not that reputation alone suffices.

\textbf{A compromised producer is unmeasured.} Producers are trusted about their
own services throughout.

\textbf{The encoder guard is a trade, not a win.} Refusing to pool calibration
scores across routers running different encoders yields lower satisfaction and
lower realised error than pooling them naively ($0.948$/$0.023$ against
$0.951$/$0.027$ at a quarter heterogeneous). For an error-budget system that
trade is defensible, but it is not a clean improvement.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Sharing a verified resolution by gossip, rather than caching it per router,
keeps recognition cost from growing with the network---by an amount we bound
with the two conditions we measured rather than assumed. The same verification
signal that makes sharing safe also lets a forwarding plane generate its own
calibration labels and audit its own peers. Those labels are biased in a way we
traced to a missing distinction in NDN's Nack, and the regime in which
correcting that bias helps inverts on a quantity operators cannot observe. We
report that as an open problem with its magnitude measured, together with two
mechanisms that did not survive their own evaluation, because a design space is
described as much by what fails in it as by what works.
